Last updated: October 31, 2026

Many project pages on this site, and the short videos that go with them, show a savings figure. Savings shown are estimates based on an approximate conventional dig-and-replace, which includes collateral costs such as re-landscaping, repaving, structural rebuilds, porches, stairs and similar restoration. Actual costs vary by property. This page explains what goes into that estimate and what it is not.

The two numbers being compared

  • The lining price. What the trenchless repair shown on the page cost, including the work the page describes: cleaning, camera inspection, the liner or new pipe, reinstating branch connections, and the access pit and its restoration.
  • The approximate dig-and-replace cost. An estimate of what a conventional open-trench replacement of the same run would have cost. It is not a quote from any company. It covers the digging itself (equipment, crew, trench shoring, hauling and backfill, new pipe, permits and inspections) plus the collateral costs a trench brings: re-landscaping and tree removal; repaving driveways, walkways and patios; structural rebuilds where the pipe runs under a slab, porch, stairs or other structure; and any street or sidewalk restoration the city requires.

Estimated savings is the approximate dig-and-replace cost minus the lining price. The percentage shown is that difference divided by the dig estimate. Every savings figure on this site is an estimate for that reason, and none has been independently verified.

Why dig estimates vary so much

Two sewer lines of the same length can carry very different excavation costs. A pipe under an open lawn is cheap to dig; a pipe under a driveway, a mature tree, a porch or the house slab is not, because the dig cost is mostly what sits on top of the pipe. Depth, soil, groundwater, the distance to the city main, and local permit and street-restoration rules move the number too. That is why the savings on this site are presented job by job rather than as a single rate, and why one job's figure should not be assumed to apply to another property.

What this means for your own project

Use these pages to understand the kind of difference trenchless methods can make and what drives it, not as a price for your pipe. The only way to know what your repair will cost, by either method, is a camera inspection and a written estimate for your property. Every installer listed here provides estimates free of charge, and if you have been quoted a dig-and-replace, a second opinion from a trenchless contractor costs nothing.
